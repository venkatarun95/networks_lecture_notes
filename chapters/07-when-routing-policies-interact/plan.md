# Chapter 7 plan

**Opening problem.** Pick up Chapter 6's last question. Two operators can honor their offers while changing which ones they accept. Give the network every chance to settle by holding links, origins, rankings, and export rules fixed. Ask readers what quantity must make progress.

1. Use Lecture 8's three ASes routing to AS 0. Have readers choose paths, then trace the changes as announcements arrive. Prove no stable assignment exists by asking which ASes could keep their direct paths.
2. Reverse one ranking. Follow the proof from the AS that can commit first to those whose choices it fixes. Use a second small example to distinguish a stable assignment from convergence under all fair update orders.
3. Try a global registry, then derive the simpler commercial rules from the promises in Chapter 6. Draw the customer–provider graph, and use the order of dependencies to motivate Gao and Rexford's sufficient condition. Use path exploration to connect the guarantee to measurements after a withdrawal.
4. Study the difference between stability and reachability through incorrect offers and withdrawals. Use the YouTube incident and the Facebook DNS outage to ask what operators can infer at different vantage points.
5. Close with the 2024 final's two policy-design problems and a lab that lets readers change rankings and update order. Turn a finite simulation trace into a question that calls for a proof.
