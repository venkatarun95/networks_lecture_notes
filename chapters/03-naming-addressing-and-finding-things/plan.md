# Chapter 3 writing plan · Naming, Addressing, and Finding Things

Chapter 2 let Returno forward a packet once somebody had filled in the addresses and forwarding tables. Pick up the story at a Cooville laptop that has just joined the village network. Its owner knows the name of a website across the valley. Follow the first visit from that laptop, letting each step supply a piece of information that the next step needs.

1. Let the owner try typing a familiar name. Work backward just far enough to discover why the laptop needs configuration before it can ask anyone about the name. Show what the laptop learns on joining a typical IPv4 network, who supplies it, and how its first request can reach a local helper while it still lacks an assigned address. Draw a short exchange the reader can refer back to.
2. Follow the query to the configured DNS resolver. On a cache miss, trace the referrals for one name, then repeat the visit while the answer is cached. Explain why the village can still exchange packets if the resolver is down. Keep the website's name distinct from the addresses of the people answering questions about it.
3. Follow the first packet addressed to the website. Use one small subnet and a concrete address to explain a shared binary prefix and the laptop's choice between local delivery and the gateway. Show how it finds the next hop's link address and where link addresses change along a path. Give flat addressing a fair try before asking what a distant router would have to remember.
4. Give the far-side router a small table with a default, a broad prefix, and an exception. Have readers predict a next hop by hand, then draw the same search as a binary tree. Compress adjacent entries with the same next hop and test the boundary addresses and the exception. Put the reader in a position to discover when an apparent saving changes delivery.
5. Finish the visit at the server, where the port directs traffic to the appropriate service; trace enough of the return path to distinguish simultaneous connections. Revisit address allocation in Cooville now that the reader knows what a neighboring router can summarize. Use the verified quiz examples for practice and include at least one symptom the reader can diagnose using the journey they just followed.

Check Lectures 1, 4 and 22 individually and inspect the cited 2024 and 2025 quiz pages before adapting them. Keep the source locations and any invented packet traces in notes. After drafting, apply the supplied cohesion prompt paragraph by paragraph, especially at the transition from hostname lookup to forwarding. Keep the diagrams and explanatory prose in the same order a reader encounters the decisions. Build and verify the chapter.
