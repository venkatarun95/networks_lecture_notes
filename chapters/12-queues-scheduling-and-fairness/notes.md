# Chapter 12 source notes

- [Lecture 13, lines 19442–19553](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L19442) supplies the overloaded output and the question of who is hurt by retry traffic. [Lecture 14, lines 21697–21757 and 21862–21926](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L21697) links a standing queue to rising round-trip time and works through buffer effects on AIMD. The chapter assumes the congestion-control derivations in Chapter 11.
- [Lecture 17, lines 26645–26748](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L26645) introduces FIFO and tail drop. A student suggests replacing the head for a stream where stale data is less valuable. Lines 26753–26865 give bursty web transfers and varying wireless service. Lines 26866–27056 discuss large buffers, TCP timing and the active file downloader delaying sparse web traffic. The six-packet burst and shared-exit wait convention are new.
- [Lecture 17, lines 27063–27243](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L27063) gives flow classification, per-flow isolation, work conservation and bounded hardware queues with possible hash collisions. The numerical timelines, ten-connection comparison, weights and priority exercise are new. [RFC 8290, §4.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8290.html#section-4.2) motivates the byte-credit mechanism. The book describes a basic DRR rule, so its rules for credit and active lists are simpler than FQ-CoDel's.
- [Lecture 17, lines 27223–27314](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L27223) explains limiting traffic at the network edge and gives the finite token-bucket example. The numerical burst bound is new. [RFC 2475, §2.3.3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2475.html#section-2.3.3) distinguishes delaying traffic to fit the profile from policing. [RFC 7567, §§2–4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7567.html) supports the AQM and ECN extension.
- The rendered [Quiz 2 2026, Question 1, pp. 1–2](../../assets/quiz2-2026.pdf#page=1) shows three customers using S3's output port 4 to S4, Steam's 1,000-byte packets, 40,000 bytes as switch-wide storage, a 2 Mbit/s link, and 7 ms of baseline trip. The adapted figure keeps those paths and the bottleneck. Actual Steam queue depths depend on the reader's A2 code, so the adaptation supplies a hypothetical bound on all work ahead, including partial service.
- [Lecture 21, lines 31918–32025](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L31918) introduces the planned-but-unasked InstaPoll and goes through equal throughput, airtime and the sum-throughput objective. The rendered [Quiz 2 2026, Question 2, pp. 2–4](../../assets/quiz2-2026.pdf#page=2) supplies 60 and 6 Mbit/s, the increase to 12 Mbit/s, and the packet alternation exercise. The 4 Mbit/s floor is new. The figure redraws the resource allocation as airtime rather than reusing the AP/rate topology.
- The rendered [Quiz 2 2026, Question 5, pp. 7–10](../../assets/quiz2-2026.pdf#page=7) supplies the two-port equal-speed switch and three scheduler placements. [Lecture 21, lines 32755–32874](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L32755) identifies two counterexamples. One assigns separate outputs to very different numbers of flows, the other gives queues with different populations a matching-level share. The manuscript gives explicit rates. It leaves crossbar mechanics to Chapter 15. The arbitrarily large ratio claim uses the idealized model's unbounded flow population.
