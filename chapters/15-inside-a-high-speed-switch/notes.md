# Chapter 15 source notes

- [Lecture 17, lines 27392–27538](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L27392) contains the classroom loopback TCP demonstration, the comments about syscall/kernel time, and the questions about hardware logic and parallelism. Its measured speed is machine- and workload-specific. The chapter's 100 Gbit/s and 100-byte service calculation is a separate illustrative budget, not a measured result.
- [Lecture 17, lines 27566–27758](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L27566) begins with shared DRAM, separates input from output directions at one physical port, follows an ingress lookup, and derives crosspoint selection as a bipartite matching. Distinguishing physical crosspoints from the demand graph and maximum from maximal matching is explicit in the manuscript. The small shared snapshot and figures are new.
- [Lecture 17, lines 27772–27817](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L27772) elicits the output-buffer argument and a student question about whether a slower downstream link could tie up fabric time. The manuscript states aligned slot and always-ready output assumptions and distinguishes finite staging from standing backlog.
- [Lecture 17, lines 27919–28065](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L27919) asks the class what could go wrong with FIFO input queues and derives virtual output queues. The section on partitioned storage records the student explanation about unused memory stranded at idle queues. The explicit bandwidth accounting distinguishes aggregate reads and writes from a one-direction line rate. Lines 28194–28257 treat output queuing and programmable stages. The chapter omits time-sensitive hardware comparisons.
- The rendered [Quiz 2 2025, Q12–Q15, pp. 6–7](../../assets/quiz2-2025.pdf#page=6) is the source of the 16-port, 100-byte and 1 μs conditions, the maximum/minimum output and the placement of unbounded backlog. The last part on fabric speedup and its slot accounting are new. The lab runs equal-sized units, one transfer per fabric edge, and empty egress queues.
- The rendered [Quiz 2 2026, Question 5, pp. 7–10](../../assets/quiz2-2026.pdf#page=7) depicts the two-port VOQ crossbar and asks where to enforce per-flow fairness. [Lecture 21, lines 32755–32874](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L32755) discusses the ratio counterexamples. Chapter 12 treats these quantitatively. The chapter 15 figure instead shows exactly which requests are visible before arbitration.
- [McKeown et al., §§I–II](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/papers/mckeown02.pdf) is the supporting primary source for VOQ scheduling and the distinction between throughput and the choice of matching objective. The text avoids inferring durable fairness merely from maximizing per-slot cardinality.
