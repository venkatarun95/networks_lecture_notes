# Chapter 19 source notes

- [Lecture 23, lines 35876–36030](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L35876) introduces buildings with industrial power and cooling, rack hardware and top-of-rack switches. The opening departs from Chapter 18's browser-visible API delay and invents three workers and timestamps to follow it. [Lines 37342–37383](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L37342) supply the class comparison between 128 cores on one physical machine and 32 cores each on four. Rack failure domains are an adaptation.
- [Lecture 23, lines 36035–36173](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L36035) asks students to choose facility locations. It discusses proximity to people, electricity and land, interconnect sites, and ping observations whose responder cannot necessarily be placed with certainty. The 2,000-km lower bound uses Chapter 13's estimate of 200,000 km/s in fiber. No assumption that a particular named host remains in its historical location is needed.
- [Lecture 23, lines 36175–36364](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L36175) discusses public and private deployments, maintenance, short links, and short-term demand for a thousand machines during a large sale. [Lines 36684–36779](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L36684) add separately managed storage, replication and failed hardware. The holiday example is new. Storage replicas can share failure modes, so the text avoids absolute guarantees.
- [Lecture 23, lines 36531–36689](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L36531) develops the social feed versus all-to-all dissemination question. The course connects it to distributing link-state updates to every router. The quadratic comparison is between workloads with stated per-user and delivery assumptions. The distributed alternatives and timetable framing are new.
- [Lecture 23, lines 36826–37139](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L36826) contrasts a data center operator's control with interdomain negotiations, asks for topology requirements, and uses a tree, then a fat-tree with path choices. The small redraw represents the single-switch-per-stage contrast, same hop count and multiple equal-length paths. See also [Lecture 9, lines 14132–14168](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L14132) and the primary account of [early Google fabrics](https://research.google/pubs/jupiter-rising-a-decade-of-clos-topologies-and-centralized-control-in-googles-datacenter-network-2/). Rack cut capacities and the failure exercise are new.
- [Lecture 23, lines 37095–37249](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L37095) covers packet spraying, three-spine hashing, large and small flow distributions, and repicking routes. The uniform-hash exercise, avoided provider-specific claims, and conditional gap-based switching are editorial choices. Rendered and inspected [Final 2024, Q7–Q9, p. 5](../../assets/cs356-final-2024.pdf#page=5), including the six-server and three-router diagram and the three-duplicate-ACK assumption. Chapter 8 adapts Q7 more directly.
- [Lecture 23, lines 36652–36689 and 37228–37259](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L37228) motivates specialized training networks with predictable gradient exchanges. The fluid 20 μs incast calculation and four-worker collective are new examples. The example uses kB in decimal units and assumes initially empty queues. [Vasudevan et al., §§1–2](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~dga/papers/incast-sigcomm2009.pdf) supply experimental evidence for synchronized high fan-in and timer delays from missing replies.
- Rendered and inspected [Quiz 2, 2026, Q4, pp. 6–7](../../assets/quiz2-2026.pdf#page=6), including four group switches and their four cores. The quiz gives paired workloads, instantaneous optimal routing, and link rates. Chapter 11 uses that assumption to derive a congestion window. This chapter proves the route assignment under the simplified topology, then changes the workload to shared destination contention. None of the matching argument is presented as a deployed controller design.
- [Lecture 23, lines 37261–37383, 37389–37496, and 37545–37575](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L37261) supplies the firewall and encryption questions, performance variation, VM placement, loopback demonstration and NIC offload discussion. The chapter uses the loopback as a diagnostic without relying on the specific throughput stated in the subtitles. The tenant identifier and reservation-after-a-failure exercises are new. The Lecture 23 InstaPoll was about lecture coverage, [lines 37515–37544](../../assets/f26-subtitles.txt#L37515).
