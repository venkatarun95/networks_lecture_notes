# Chapter 6 plan

**Opening problem.** Two providers in Austin can avoid a detour through Houston by connecting directly. Put the reader at the far end of the link and ask whose traffic they would agree to carry. Chapter 5's common map gives way to choices made by independent operators.

1. Follow the lecture's proposal for a common core. Let the class's failure, ownership and geography questions motivate autonomous systems and exchange points.
2. Invite designs for expressing preferences in a packet or exchanging them up front. Use the grocery-store oranges to motivate exchanging offers of reachability instead, then introduce the BGP prefix, AS path, next hop and withdrawal.
3. Use the lecture's two regional carriers, common upstream and three customers to trace announcements for customer R. Ask whether A should export B's offer to its own provider. Derive a table from the business agreements. Give separate drawings for relationship direction and update direction.
4. Revisit Chapter 5's hidden loop dependency using a path that contains the receiver's number. Introduce AS-path prepending as an intentional repeat. Use a multihomed enterprise to distinguish shorter paths from preferred paths and outbound choices from inbound influence. Apply the final's NTP question to fixed asymmetric routes.
5. Move from two upstreams to two handoffs with the same upstream. Use Seattle and Austin to distinguish the exit decision from the IGP work needed to reach that exit.
6. Run through the lecture's traceroute discussion and adapt the InstaPoll's three packet-drop cases. End by asking whether local changes in route choice and export can keep the conversation going indefinitely.

The Chapter 6 exercises work from the printed diagrams. The following chapter can use the 2024 quiz topology set to address whether policy combinations converge.
